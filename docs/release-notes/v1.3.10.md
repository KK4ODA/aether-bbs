**Aether BBS checked against Aether HF 0.2.0-beta.168: it no longer takes Aether's host port from other programs, and an Aether HF that is busy no longer leaves the BBS on the wrong bandwidth.**

## Aether HF

- **The BBS no longer takes Aether HF's host port from another program.** Aether HF serves
  one host program at a time and gives the port to the newest connection. When Winlink
  Express, VarAC or RMS Trimode attached to the same Aether HF, the BBS reconnected within
  seconds and cut it off; with RMS Trimode, which reconnects too, the two traded the port
  forever. Now the BBS asks Aether's control API before it attaches or reattaches. While
  another program holds the port, it logs *Another program holds Aether HF's host port*, waits,
  and attaches once the port is free.
- **A refused bandwidth is asked for again.** With *Bandwidth* set to 500 or 2300 Hz, Aether HF
  refuses `BW` while a session, call or transmission (a beacon, an APRS datagram) is under way,
  and then runs its own bandwidth. The BBS logged *Modem may NOT be listening* and stayed on the
  wrong bandwidth until its next restart. Now it says *Aether HF could not move to … Hz now* and
  asks again every 10 seconds until Aether moves. Meanwhile it places no outbound call: bulletin
  forwarding waits for its next interval instead of calling in the wrong bandwidth.
- **No false "not answering calls" warning.** Aether's `answering` reading is about its own
  callsign. With the BBS attached under another one (Aether as KK4ODA, the BBS as KK4ODA-10),
  the card warned *Aether HF is not answering calls (LISTEN is off)* for good, although calls to
  the BBS were answered. The warning now reads what the BBS itself holds on Aether's host port.
  It shows when that program is not listening, or when it does not hold the BBS callsign, which
  happens when another program holds the port or the callsign.
- **Stopping an Aether HF the BBS started waits for its session to end on the air.** Aether
  sends its disconnect and closing Morse identifier first (up to 22 seconds). The BBS waited
  12 seconds, then ended the whole process: the identifier could be cut, and a radio keyed over
  CAT or rigctld could stay keyed. It now waits 30 seconds, as Aether HF's own window does.
- **Scanning holds the frequency from `PENDING`.** Aether answers a call (`PTT ON`) before it
  says `CONNECTED`, which can come up to 12 seconds later. The scanner kept stepping until
  `CONNECTED`, so the answer could go out on the wrong channel. Scanning now stops at `PENDING`
  and resumes on `CANCELPENDING`, at the end of the session, or 30 seconds after a `PENDING`
  that led nowhere. This applies to VARA too.
- **A call Aether refuses with `WRONG` ends at once.** Aether answers `CONNECT` to a callsign it
  cannot carry (more than nine characters, or characters other than letters, digits, `-` and
  `/`) with `WRONG` and no `DISCONNECTED`. Bulletin forwarding waited the full three minutes;
  now it logs *the modem refused the call* and moves on.
- **Forwarding waits up to three minutes for `DISCONNECTED`.** Aether sends what is queued (a
  minute at most) and its Morse identifier before it reports the end of a session; the BBS
  waited one minute.
- `docs/AETHER_HF.md` is updated for all of this, and for beacons: Aether
  refuses them under automatic control only while a rules profile is in force.
